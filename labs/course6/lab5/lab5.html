Lab 5 – Use a Playbook to Respond to a Phishing Incident

Course 6 - Sound the Alarm: Detection and Response

Overview

In this activity, I responded to a phishing incident involving a known malicious file hash. I used a phishing incident response playbook to guide my actions, followed the documented steps to evaluate the alert, and updated the alert ticket with my findings. This demonstrates how I apply structured procedures to handle phishing alerts in a SOC environment.

Scenario

As a level-one SOC analyst at a financial services company, I received an alert about a possible phishing attempt. The alert indicated that an employee had received an email with a password‑protected attachment and may have opened it. The attachment, bfsvc.exe, was associated with a SHA256 hash that had already been verified as malicious in a previous investigation.

My task was to follow the organization’s phishing incident response playbook, complete the investigation, and update the alert ticket with a clear summary of the incident, including whether the attachment was malicious and what actions should be taken next.

Phishing Incident Response Playbook

The phishing playbook provides a step-by-step process for handling phishing alerts. It ensures that level-one analysts respond consistently and escalate incidents appropriately when malicious activity is confirmed. The main steps in the playbook are:

  • Step 1: Receive phishing alert
  • Step 2: Evaluate the alert (severity, sender/receiver details, subject, body, links, attachments)
  • Step 3.0–3.2: Determine if links/attachments exist, assess if they are malicious, and escalate if needed
  • Step 4: Close the alert ticket if no malicious content is found

Applying the Playbook to the Alert

Step 1: Receive phishing alert

I began with the alert ticket indicating a possible phishing attempt:

Ticket ID Alert Message Severity Status
A-2703 SERVER-MAIL Phishing attempt possible download of malware Medium Open

Step 2: Evaluate the alert

I reviewed the alert details, including sender and receiver information, the subject line, and the message body. The email claimed to be a job application and instructed the recipient to open a password‑protected attachment using the provided password. This is a common phishing tactic used to bypass basic security controls and convince users to execute malicious files.

The attachment was identified as bfsvc.exe, and the associated SHA256 hash 54e6ea47eb04634d3e87fd7787e2136ccfbcc80ade34f246a12cf93bab527f6b was already known to be malicious from a previous VirusTotal analysis.

Step 3: Confirm malicious content and escalate

Following the playbook, I confirmed that the email contained an attachment and that the attachment was malicious. VirusTotal reported that the file hash was detected by 54 out of 71 vendors and associated with the FlagPro backdoor malware, exhibiting anti‑debugging and lateral movement behaviors. Based on this, the playbook required escalation.

I updated the ticket comments to summarize the findings and changed the ticket status to Escalated so that a level-two SOC analyst could continue with containment and remediation actions.

Step 4: Documenting the ticket

I documented the known malicious hash, the email details (sender, recipient, subject, and attachment), and the reason for escalation. Clear documentation ensures that higher-level analysts have all the necessary context to respond quickly and effectively.

Reflection

This activity reinforced how incident response playbooks help standardize phishing investigations and reduce response time. By following the documented steps, I was able to evaluate the alert, confirm the malicious attachment using threat intelligence, and properly escalate the incident with clear, well‑structured documentation.
