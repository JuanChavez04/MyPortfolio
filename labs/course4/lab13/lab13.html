Lab 13 – Applying More filters in SQL

Course 4 - Tools of Trade: Linux and SQL

Overview

In this lab, I practiced using the AND, OR, and NOT operators to filter SQL query results. These operators allow for more precise data retrieval and are essential for analyzing employee information, login activity, and department‑level data in relational databases.

Task 1 – Filter for Login Attempts That Occurred After Hours

I used the AND operator to filter login attempts that occurred after 18:00 and were unsuccessful. This type of filtering is useful for identifying suspicious activity outside normal business hours.

Task 2 – Filter for Login Attempts on Specific Dates

I used the OR operator to return login attempts that occurred on either May 8 or May 9. This demonstrated how to retrieve records that match multiple conditions.

Task 3 – Filter for Login Attempts from Specific Locations

I used the NOT operator to exclude login attempts from countries starting with “MEX”. This is useful when focusing on activity from all other regions.

Task 4 – Filter for Employees in Specific Departments

I used the AND operator to filter employees who work in the Marketing department and whose office is located in the East region.

Task 5 – Filter for Employees in Finance or Sales

I used the OR operator to return employees who work in either the Finance or Sales departments. This is helpful when analyzing multiple related groups.

Task 6 – Filter for Employees Not in a Specific Department

I used the NOT operator to exclude employees in the Information Technology department. This returned all employees in other departments.

Reflection

This lab strengthened my understanding of SQL logical operators and how to combine conditions to retrieve precise datasets. These filtering techniques are essential for analyzing employee information, monitoring login activity, and supporting cybersecurity investigations.
