Lab 3 – Determine appropriate data handling practices

Course 5 - Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

Overview

In this activity, I reviewed a data leak incident and evaluated whether the organization followed appropriate data handling practices. The goal was to identify what went wrong, determine which controls were relevant, and recommend improvements based on the principle of least privilege. This activity reinforces how proper access control and data‑sharing procedures help prevent accidental exposure of sensitive information.

Scenario

The scenario describes an educational technology company that experienced a data leak involving internal business plans. A sales representative accidentally shared a link to an internal folder instead of approved promotional materials. The external business partner then posted the link publicly, assuming it was intended for distribution. An audit is underway to determine how to prevent similar incidents in the future.

Incident Review

I analyzed the data leak worksheet to understand the contributing factors, the relevant NIST control (AC‑6), and how least privilege could have prevented the incident. The worksheet outlines the issues, control requirements, and recommendations for improving data handling practices.

Analysis

Several issues contributed to the data leak:

  • Excessive access permissions: The internal folder remained accessible to the entire sales team even after the meeting.
  • Lack of verification: The sales representative did not confirm the link before sharing it externally.
  • No automated safeguards: The system did not prevent external sharing of internal‑only documents.

The worksheet references NIST SP 800‑53 AC‑6 (Least Privilege), which requires limiting user access to only what is necessary for their job duties. It also emphasizes auditing permissions, preventing privilege escalation, and enforcing strict access controls.

Recommended improvements include:

  • Implementing role‑based access controls (RBAC)
  • Restricting external sharing of internal folders
  • Requiring approval workflows for link sharing
  • Automatically labeling confidential data
  • Regularly auditing user privileges

These changes would reduce the likelihood of accidental exposure and ensure that employees can only share approved materials.

Security Plan Snapshot

The worksheet also includes a security plan snapshot showing how the incident relates to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF). The relevant function is Protect, under the category PR.DS: Data Security, specifically PR.DS‑5: Protections against data leaks. This ties the incident directly to established industry standards.

Reflection

This activity highlighted the importance of enforcing least privilege and implementing strong data handling procedures. Even well‑intentioned employees can accidentally leak sensitive information if access controls are too broad or if verification steps are missing. By strengthening access restrictions, adding approval workflows, and improving oversight, organizations can significantly reduce the risk of data leaks.
