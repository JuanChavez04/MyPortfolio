Lab 1 – Classifying Assets in a Home Network

Course 5 - Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

Overview

In this activity, I reviewed a home network environment and classified each connected asset based on its sensitivity, ownership, and level of network access. Asset classification is an essential part of asset management because it helps identify which devices require stronger security controls and closer monitoring.

Scenario

The scenario describes a typical home office network with multiple devices connected to it. Each device represents a potential entry point into the network, so understanding what assets exist and how sensitive they are is critical for protecting information and reducing risk. My task was to examine the asset inventory and classify each device appropriately.

Asset Inventory and Classification

I reviewed the provided home network asset inventory and evaluated each device based on:

  • Network access level – how often the device connects
  • Ownership – who controls or uses the device
  • Location – whether the device is on‑premises or mobile
  • Notes – additional context such as stored data or functionality
  • Sensitivity – the classification level based on risk and exposure

Analysis

After reviewing the inventory, I identified the following key points:

  • High‑sensitivity assets such as the network router and Xbox contain confidential information or provide continuous access to the network, making them higher‑risk targets.
  • Restricted assets like the desktop store private personal data and require strong access controls.
  • Internal‑only assets such as the smart TV and thermostat still pose risks because they store login information or reveal occupancy patterns.
  • Guest devices introduce additional risk because they are not owned by the homeowner and may connect from off‑premises locations.

Reflection

This activity reinforced the importance of maintaining an accurate asset inventory and classifying devices based on sensitivity and risk. Even in a home network, devices vary widely in how much access they have and what information they store. Proper classification helps determine which assets require stronger protections, monitoring, or restricted access.
