Lab 6 – Improving authentication, authorization, and accounting for a small business

Course 5 - Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

Overview

In this activity, I analyzed an access control incident involving unauthorized use of an administrative account. The goal was to review the event log, identify authentication and authorization issues, and recommend improvements to strengthen access controls. This exercise demonstrates how proper authentication, authorization, and accounting (AAA) practices help prevent misuse of privileged accounts.

Scenario

A suspicious deposit was made from the business to an unknown bank account. The finance manager confirmed they did not initiate the transaction. An access log revealed that a payroll event was created using the Legal\Administrator account, even though payroll actions should only be performed by finance personnel. My task was to review the event log and complete the access control worksheet to determine what went wrong.

Event Log Review

I examined the event log to gather key details about the incident, including who performed the action, when it occurred, and what system was used. These details help identify the source of unauthorized activity and determine whether proper access controls were in place.

Access Control Analysis

Using the worksheet, I documented the issues and analyzed the organization’s current authentication and authorization practices. Key findings include:

  • Who caused the incident? The payroll event was created using the Legal\Administrator account.
  • When and where did it occur? The event occurred on 10/03/2023 at 8:29:57 AM from computer Up2-NoGud with IP 152.207.255.255.
  • What system was used? The event source was AdsmEmployeeService, which handles payroll functions.

Identified Issues

Based on the event details, I identified several authorization and authentication issues:

  • The Legal department’s Administrator account was used to perform payroll actions, violating the principles of least privilege and separation of duties.
  • The Administrator account may be shared or improperly managed, increasing the risk of unauthorized access.
  • The account may still be active even though it should not have access to payroll systems.

Recommendations

To prevent similar incidents, I recommended the following improvements:

  • Implement role-based access control (RBAC) so only finance personnel can access payroll systems.
  • Remove or restrict administrative privileges from non-finance users.
  • Require multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all administrator-level accounts.
  • Disable shared accounts and require unique credentials for every user.
  • Conduct regular access reviews to ensure inactive or cross-department accounts are deactivated.

Worksheet Evidence

Reflection

This activity reinforced the importance of strong authentication and authorization controls. Misuse of privileged accounts can lead to financial loss, fraud, and operational disruption. By enforcing least privilege, auditing access regularly, and eliminating shared accounts, organizations can significantly reduce the risk of unauthorized activity and improve their overall security posture.
