Lab 2 – Score risks based on their likelihood and severity

Course 5 - Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

Overview

In this activity, I practiced evaluating risks by scoring their likelihood and severity. This process is a key part of risk assessment and helps organizations prioritize which vulnerabilities require immediate attention. By reviewing the bank’s operational environment and analyzing each risk, I calculated priority scores that indicate how urgently each issue should be addressed.

Scenario

The scenario describes a commercial bank located in a coastal area with low crime rates. The bank has 100 on‑premises employees, 20 remote employees, and serves over 2,000 individual and 200 commercial customers. Because the bank handles sensitive financial data and must comply with strict regulations, it is essential to identify and prioritize risks that could impact operations, customer information, or financial stability.

Risk Register Review

I analyzed the provided risk register, which lists several risks affecting the bank’s primary asset: its funds. Each risk includes a description, likelihood score, severity score, and calculated priority. The priority score is determined using the formula:

Priority = Likelihood × Severity

This scoring method helps determine which risks pose the greatest threat and should be addressed first.

Analysis

After reviewing the risk register, I identified several important insights:

  • Business email compromise has the highest priority score (9), making it the most urgent risk. Human error and phishing attempts significantly increase the likelihood of this threat.
  • Compromised user database and financial records leak both have moderate priority scores (6). These risks involve poor encryption and publicly accessible backup servers, which could expose sensitive customer data.
  • Theft and supply chain disruption have lower priority scores (2), but still represent operational risks that should be monitored.

The notes section explains why these risks are possible in the bank’s environment. With many employees handling sensitive data and external partners involved in operations, the bank is exposed to human error, phishing, and supply chain issues. Its coastal location also increases the likelihood of natural disruptions.

Reflection

This activity reinforced how important it is to evaluate risks using a structured scoring system. By assigning likelihood and severity values, organizations can make informed decisions about where to allocate resources. High‑priority risks such as business email compromise require immediate mitigation, while lower‑priority risks can be addressed through long‑term planning. Understanding how to score and prioritize risks is essential for building effective security strategies.
